# Gloomhaven class retirement stories: Groups 1 and 2

Shown on the retirement screen above the rewards, the first time that class retires in a campaign. Source: tools/gh_story.py (2026-10-27).

## Group 1 (released: Brute, Scoundrel, Sunkeeper, Sawbones, Quartermaster)

### Brute: Brute Retires

You find the Brute at the main gate at dawn, pack strapped on and shield across his back. He has been quieter than usual these past weeks, which for the Brute is saying something.

"Job's done," he says when you ask. "Came north for the pay. Stayed for..." He gestures at the wall, the gate, the people hauling lumber behind you, and leaves it at that.

He unslings the shield and props it against the gatehouse. "Guard can use it more than I can." A long pause. "Nothing wrong with a wall that holds."

You ask where he will go. He shrugs toward the south: the Frozen Pass, and past it a long walk home to the Great Grove, or to Gloomhaven, or wherever the next quiet place is. You point out he has never been good at quiet. For the first time you can remember, the Brute laughs.

He walks out through the gate without looking back. Behind you, the guards take turns trying to lift the shield.

### Scoundrel: Scoundrel Retires

Nobody sees the Scoundrel leave. You find out the way you were always going to find out: her bunk is stripped, the lock on the Trading Post's back door has been picked and relocked out of sheer habit, and on your table sits a small, neat pile of everything she ever lifted from you. A coin purse. A whetstone. The good knife.

There is a note under the knife, in handwriting far better than she ever let on. "Nothing personal. It never was. Thanks for the company; it was more than I came for."

The guard at the gate swears he saw nothing all night. He is probably telling the truth. She was always better than the people watching for her.

Weeks later a caravan master mentions a woman in dark leather who paid her way south and spent the whole trip teaching the drivers to cheat at cards. You find you are smiling. Somewhere out there, the Scoundrel is doing fine.

### Sunkeeper: Sunkeeper Retires

The Sunkeeper asks you to meet her on the wall at sunrise. She is already there when you arrive, armor polished to a mirror, watching the light come up over the ice. "The temple has sent for me," she says. "They need a keeper, and there is no one else."

She does not pretend to be sorry to go, and you respect her for it. Instead she walks the length of the wall with you, laying a gloved hand on each tower as she passes, speaking a few words under her breath. The guards on duty stand a little straighter without quite knowing why.

At the gate she presses a small sun medallion into your palm, warm from her hand. "For the dark months. It will not do much. It will do enough."

Then she turns south and walks into the sunrise, which is exactly how she would have wanted it to look.

### Sawbones: Sawbones Retires

You find the Sawbones in the Alchemist's back room, labeling jars. He has been at it for days: every salve, splint and tincture he carried, written up in his cramped hand and shelved where anyone can find it.

"Enough people know how to break things in this town," he says without looking up. "Somebody ought to know how to put them back together when I am gone." He closes the last jar, wipes his hands, and finally turns around. "The sanctuary wrote. A quiet post, inland. Warm. I said yes before I could talk myself out of it."

He leaves you his saw. Not as a weapon, he insists, though you both know how he used it. "For the lumber. Honestly."

The morning he goes, half the outpost turns out to see him off, every one of them someone he has stitched, set or sobered up. He pretends to be annoyed about it the whole way to the gate.

### Quartermaster: Quartermaster Retires

The Quartermaster spends his last week in Frosthaven doing inventory. Not his own: the outpost's. By the end of it every storeroom has a ledger, every ledger has a system, and the Craftsman has stopped arguing and started taking notes.

"A party without supplies is a funeral with extra steps," he tells you over the last of his good wine. "You lot will be fine. You have the ledgers now." He taps the stack like a man patting a dog.

He has a contract waiting in the Capital, outfitting a merchant house, and he is honest enough to admit the pay is obscene. He is also honest enough to look back at the walls for a long moment before he goes.

His pack is the last thing to leave, strapped to a sled and roped like a siege engine. The Craftsman swears the storerooms have never been so tidy, and never will be again.

## Group 2 (Cragheart, Plagueherald, Nightshroud, Berserker)

### Cragheart: Cragheart Retires

The Cragheart has been sitting on the same boulder outside the north wall for three days. Nobody thought much of it; the Savvas sits still the way other people breathe. On the fourth morning the boulder is gone, and so is a long stretch of the rubble that the last attack left along the palisade. In its place stands a wall: raw, ugly, and a good deal thicker than the old one.

"It was in the way," the Cragheart says when you find them at the gate, as if that explains the week of work. They have never wasted a word on anything a stone could say for them.

They are going back to the mountains, they tell you, slowly, the way they say everything. Not to the Savvas, who would not have them, but to the high country past the Frozen Pass, where the rock is older and quieter and nobody asks it to fight.

They lay a hand flat against the new wall before they leave, as if listening. Whatever it says satisfies them. The Cragheart walks north, and the ground under the gate does not shake once.

### Plagueherald: Plagueherald Retires

The Alchemist finds you first, which tells you something has gone wrong before she says a word. "Your Harrower friend," she says, "has been in my stillroom all night. I would like them out of it. I would also like to know what they left in the third jar from the end."

You find the Plagueherald on the roof of the Alchemist's, the swarm settled over them like a shawl, watching the sun come up over the bay. They do not turn around. "The jar is a cure," they say. "For the cough the lumber crews have. She will work it out in a month; I have saved her the month."

They are leaving. The hive has decided, they explain, and a Harrower does not argue with the hive. Somewhere warmer, somewhere with more rot to feed on; they say it the way someone else would mention the weather.

The swarm lifts off the roof all at once, a dark sheet that folds and is gone over the water. The cough is gone from the crews by the end of the week. The Alchemist still will not open the third jar.

### Nightshroud: Nightshroud Retires

You had agreed to meet the Nightshroud at the Barracks at dusk. You arrive early. He is already there, which you only learn when he speaks from the shadow of the doorway you have been standing in for a minute.

"I am going back," he says. Not to Gloomhaven; back, in the way the Aesther mean it, to whichever fold of the world they step out of and into. He has found the edge of it here, in the long dark of the Frosthaven winter. "It is thin, this far north. I have been half through for weeks."

He hands you his knife, hilt first, the one you have watched end a dozen things before they knew they were in danger. "Keep it in the dark," he says, and for once you cannot tell whether it is advice or a joke.

Then the lamp in the doorway gutters, and when it steadies the shadow is only a shadow. The guards on the wall swear nobody passed them. For the first night in months, nothing in the outpost moves that you did not see.

### Berserker: Berserker Retires

The Berserker is in the Tavern, which is not news, and she is sober, which is. There is a bandage on her arm and a letter on the table in front of her, read so many times the folds have gone soft.

"My sister," she says, pushing it toward you. "The clan is moving the herds south before the snows. She asks if the one who ran off to get herself killed would like to come home instead." She takes the letter back before you can finish it. "She has a way with words."

You ask about the arm. She shrugs: it will heal, they always do, that was never the problem. The problem was that for a long time she did not mind whether they did. "Not any more," she says, and looks surprised to hear it out loud.

She leaves the next morning with the trade caravan, walking beside the wagons rather than riding, because sitting still was never going to happen. At the bend in the road she turns and raises the bandaged arm, once, and then she is gone into the white.
